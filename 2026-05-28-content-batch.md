## 🔍 Trend Report

Three findings dominate today's cycle. Two are regulatory countdowns the affiliate business needs to be content-positioned for; the third is a credibility opening the rest of the niche is fumbling.

**1. The PCAC clock is at 56 days, and the audience is starting to ask "what do I do *between now and then*?"** The Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee's vote on adding BPC-157, TB-500, KPV and the rest of the reclassified peptides to the formal 503A bulks list is scheduled for **July 23–24, 2026**. Today is May 28. That's 56 days. The peptides came off Category 2 on **April 23, 2026** but the bulks list addition is still pending. Reddit traffic on r/peptides and r/biohackers has shifted noticeably this week from "is it back yet?" to "what's the right way to start a cycle right now versus waiting until late July?" That's a buying-intent signal — readers in the latter mindset are pre-shopping, not just educating themselves. The content gap: nobody is publishing a clean "wait vs. start now" decision tree, with the actual variables (clinic willingness, source verification, dosing protocol stability, what changes after the July vote). That is exactly the piece that wins both organic SEO and the email-list capture.

**2. Compounded GLP-1 is bleeding out faster than the broader weight-loss space realizes — and the recovery niche should not gloat about it.** Today is 32 days from the **June 29, 2026** close of the public comment window on the FDA's proposed exclusion of semaglutide, tirzepatide, and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list. That comment window opened with the April 30 proposal. Meanwhile, the March 3 round of 30 FDA warning letters to telehealth GLP-1 marketers has already pushed major players (Hims & Hers cut its compounded semaglutide pill on February 7) out of the market. Novo Nordisk's litigation against Hims & Hers is still active. The narrative trap the recovery-peptide niche is walking into: positioning BPC/TB-500 as "the peptides that didn't get banned" makes the audience feel smart but invites the same FDA scrutiny that took down the GLP-1 compounders. The smart content frame is *narrower*: BPC-157 is moving the opposite direction because its safety/efficacy profile cleared a different bar, not because peptides as a category are winning. Don't antagonize the agency that is currently giving you something.

**3. The STAT News piece from February 3, 2026 is the credibility lever the niche is ignoring.** Most affiliate sites in this niche refuse to engage with the central counter-argument: that almost the entire BPC-157 evidence base comes from a single research group in Croatia (Sikiric et al., University of Zagreb) and that human RCT data is essentially nonexistent. STAT laid this out and the affiliate side has mostly ignored it. That refusal is bad for two reasons. First, AI search (Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT) is increasingly citing STAT-tier critical sources in answers — which means the affiliate content that doesn't engage with the critique gets cited *less*. Second, the audience that's actually buying — practitioners, longevity-aware HENRYs — can smell a one-sided pitch. The content move: directly cite STAT's critique, concede the single-research-group point, then walk through the mechanism-level reasons the existing data is still informative. Honest broker beats hype merchant on a 90-day cohort.
