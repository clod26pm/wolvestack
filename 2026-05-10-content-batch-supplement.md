## 🔍 Trend Report (Supplement)

Three findings that the earlier batch missed, in declining order of newsworthiness:

**1. The Penn / *Nature Health* GLP-1 Reddit study is the most useful pharmacovigilance paper of 2026 so far — and almost no influencer has read it correctly.** Sehgal et al., *Nature Health*, April 2026, applied a large language model to 400,000+ Reddit posts from roughly 70,000 semaglutide and tirzepatide users covering five-plus years of patient self-report. The expected GI cluster confirmed at trial-grade rates: nausea 36.9%, fatigue 16.7%, vomiting 16.3%, constipation 15.3%, diarrhea 12.6%. Two clusters emerged that are *underrepresented* in the FDA labels: thermoregulatory (chills, hot flashes — mechanistically plausible given GLP-1 receptor expression in the rostral ventrolateral medulla and known sympathetic effects), and reproductive (menstrual irregularity in roughly 4% of all users — closer to ~8% in the female fraction of the sample, since the cohort is roughly half female). The authors are emphatic: this is associative, not causal. But the more interesting question is *why* this signal showed up on Reddit and not in SUSTAIN-6 / STEP-1 / SURMOUNT-1. Answer: those trials had weight-loss and HbA1c primary endpoints. Menstrual data was not systematically collected. The instrumentation was elsewhere. Confidence: **high**. Primary source linked at the bottom of this file.

**2. The PCAC public-comment deadline is July 9, 2026, and it's the single most underused regulatory lever in the peptide space right now.** Per Federal Register Docket No. FDA-2025-N-6895: comments submitted to Regulations.gov on or before **July 9** receive priority committee distribution; comments submitted July 10–22 are still considered but at lower priority; the docket closes 11:59 PM ET on July 22. Almost nobody on TikTok knows public comment is real. Almost nobody filing comments knows that length doesn't equal weight (clinical case stories outperform anecdote; cited literature outperforms enthusiasm). This is a content angle that doubles as audience activation. Confidence: **high**.

**3. The PCAC Day-2 docket (July 24) is the sleeper story.** Day 1 (July 23) covers BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, Thymosin Alpha-1, AOD-9604 — the peptides every TikTok creator already knows. Day 2 covers Emideltide (DSIP family — delta-sleep-inducing peptide), Semax-related substances (synthetic ACTH(4–10) analog with Russian neuroprotection literature), and Epitalon-related substances (the AlaGluAspGly tetrapeptide; the only short peptide ever shown to activate telomerase in human somatic cells in vitro). The U.S. regulatory apparatus has historically refused to engage with the Soviet-era and modern Russian-language gerontology literature on Epitalon and Semax. July 24 is the first time a federal advisory committee will formally review them. The vacuum is loud; whoever publishes credible English-language coverage of those peptides this month captures the search wave. Confidence: **high on the procedural facts, moderate on the underlying clinical evidence base** — Russian-language literature requires careful translation and methodological scrutiny.

The authors are emphatic the data is associative, not causal. Reddit posters are not a representative population. But here's the question worth sitting with: *why* did this signal show up on Reddit and not in the trials?

Because the trials weren't asking. When the primary endpoint is weight, you don't track menstrual cycles. The data was there all along. The instrumentation was elsewhere.

## ⚠️ Regulatory Watch (Supplement)

Three items worth tracking through the next 60 days that the primary batch did not flag:

The PCAC public-comment deadline of **July 9, 2026** for priority-review distribution is the single most underused regulatory lever in the peptide space right now. Comments submitted to Regulations.gov on Docket FDA-2025-N-6895 by July 9 are flagged for committee distribution before the vote; comments July 10–22 still considered but at lower priority. Docket closes 11:59 PM ET on July 22. Almost no biohacker reading TikTok knows public comment is a real lever.

Day 2 of the PCAC meeting (**July 24, 2026**) is procedurally as important as Day 1, and most coverage has missed it. Substances under review on Day 2 include Emideltide-related (DSIP family), Semax-related (synthetic ACTH 4–10 analog), and Epitalon-related (the AlaGluAspGly tetrapeptide; in vitro telomerase activation). It represents the most institutionally credible negative consensus statement issued to date and is worth engaging with directly in the next cornerstone piece — agreeing with what's correct (sourcing quality is genuinely uncontrolled in the gray market), rebutting what's exaggerated (the institutional warning conflates research-grade compounded peptides with research-chemical-vendor peptides).
