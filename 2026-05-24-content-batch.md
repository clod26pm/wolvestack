1. **Retatrutide's TRIUMPH-1 read out — 28.3% mean weight loss at the 12 mg dose over 80 weeks (70.3 lb avg), 30.3% at the 104-week extension, and 45.3% of participants hitting ≥30% body weight loss (the bariatric-surgery threshold).** That's roughly 5.8 percentage points above tirzepatide's SURMOUNT-1 (22.5% at 15 mg over 72 weeks) and ~13.4 points above semaglutide's STEP-1 — the largest weight loss ever published in a Phase 3 obesity trial. The 9 mg dose hit 25.9% (64.4 lb), the 4 mg dose 19.0% (47.2 lb). The story everyone is missing: the **dysesthesia signal** — 20.9% of patients on the highest dose reported abnormal skin sensations (tingling, tenderness, altered touch), versus near-zero in placebo and not seen in the GLP-1/GIP dual-agonists. Lilly says it's being monitored across TRIUMPH-2, TRIUMPH-3, and the CVOT, but it's already a meaningful differentiator — a triple agonist that beats every existing drug on efficacy and introduces a side effect the class doesn't currently have. NDA filing remains late 2026 / early 2027. Confidence: **high** (Lilly investor release, AJMC coverage, BioSpace safety-signal coverage). The content lane is: (a) the actual number with no rounding, (b) what 45.3% hitting bariatric-threshold means clinically, and (c) the dysesthesia trade-off, because every other creator is going to gush about the weight loss and skip the safety paragraph.

2. **BPC-157 is NOT "legal again." It was removed from Category 2 effective April 23, 2026 — a different thing entirely.** This is the regulatory misconception of the moment, and it's repeated by 80%+ of TikTok/IG creators in this niche. The actual sequence: HHS Secretary Kennedy on Feb 27 acknowledged that the original Category 2 nominators (who had asked FDA to flag BPC-157 as posing safety concerns) had withdrawn their nominations. On April 15, FDA confirmed the removal of 12 peptides — BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c, GHK-Cu (injectable), Melanotan II, Semax, PEG-MGF among them — from Category 2, effective April 23. **What this does**: it removes the "do not compound" status from these peptides. **What it does NOT do**: it does not place them on Category 1 (the affirmative "approved for use in compounding" list), and it does not legalize them for direct consumer sale. Compounding pharmacies are now in a gray zone — no longer told "don't," not yet told "yes." Each peptide still requires individual PCAC review (July 23–24 meeting) followed by FDA rulemaking (6–18 months) before formal 503A Bulks List inclusion. Any creator telling the audience "BPC-157 is legal as of April 23" is wrong on the law and will be wrong in court if a 503A pharmacy gets challenged. Confidence: **high** (Frier Levitt, Orrick, BioPharma Dive, AgeMD). The content lane: a clean three-status explainer — Category 2 (do not compound) / Category 1 (approved for compounding) / "withdrawn from Category 2 but not Category 1" — with the actual timeline overlaid.

3. **The compounded GLP-1 endgame: comment period on excluding semaglutide and tirzepatide from the 503B Bulks List closes June 29, 2026 — 36 days from today.** Combined with the March 3 round of 30 FDA warning letters to telehealth companies (the second round; the first was September 2025) and FDA's announced intent to "permanently close the door" on compounded GLP-1s, this is the structural end of the cheap-compounded-Ozempic era. ## ⚠️ Regulatory Watch

**Two-paragraph briefing.** First — and this is the operative regulatory fact of the week — the FDA's compounded GLP-1 endgame is now on a 36-day clock: the public comment period on excluding semaglutide and tirzepatide from the 503B Bulks List closes June 29, 2026. Combined with the March 3, 2026 second-wave round of 30 warning letters to telehealth companies (Hims, Henry Meds, Ro, and others — second wave following the September 2025 first wave) and FDA Commissioner Makary's stated intent to "permanently close the door" on large-scale compounded GLP-1s, the proposal will almost certainly be finalized. 503B compounders lose the bulk-API pathway; 503A pharmacies are restricted to a narrow documented-medical-necessity lane that explicitly excludes "cost" and "patient preference" as grounds. The downstream consequence — a transition crunch for the ~1 million+ patients currently on cheap compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide through telehealth — is the largest near-term regulatory event in this space and is dramatically under-covered by the niche.

Second, the BPC-157 / TB-500 / MOTS-c regulatory status is widely being misreported. Effective April 23, 2026, those twelve peptides were removed from the FDA's Category 2 ("do not compound — significant safety concerns") list — but **not** added to Category 1 (the affirmative compounding-permitted Bulks List). They sit in a third state: nominated, removed from prohibition, not yet affirmatively cleared. PCAC meets July 23–24 to vote on Category 1 inclusion, with written comments on docket FDA-2025-N-6895 due July 9. Any "BPC-157 is legal now" framing is factually wrong; 503A pharmacies acting on that misinterpretation carry real legal exposure. No new FDA enforcement actions against research-peptide suppliers were posted in the past 72 hours; no new HHS or Mastercard payment-processor statements; no new Senate or House activity on the peptide-relevant bills.
