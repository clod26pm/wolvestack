# Peptide Content Batch — 2026-05-25

## 🔍 Trend Report

Three findings define the news cycle this week. Two are regulatory hinges that the entire compounded-peptide economy is rotating around; the third is a creator-side trend that is still climbing.

**1. The FDA reclassification is now in its limbo window.** On February 27, 2026, HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. announced on the Joe Rogan Experience that roughly 14 of the 19 peptides currently on the FDA's Category 2 "do-not-compound" interim list — BPC-157 among them — will be reclassified out of Category 2 to a status that allows licensed compounding pharmacies to prepare them by prescription. The administrative effective date that took them off Category 2 was **April 23, 2026**, but the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee is not scheduled to formally vote on adding them to the 503A bulks list (the actual "you can legally compound this" list) until its **July 23–24, 2026** meeting. The gap between these two dates is the entire game right now. Telehealth clinics and compounding pharmacies are quietly resuming BPC-157 prescriptions on a "no longer prohibited, not yet officially permitted" reading. Cautious patients are waiting for the July vote. None of these peptides are FDA-approved; none are over-the-counter; and the formal updated bulks list has not been published.

**2. The compounded GLP-1 door is closing faster than most patients realize.** On April 30, 2026, the FDA formally proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide, and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list — meaning outsourcing facilities would lose the legal basis to compound them from bulk active ingredient. The public comment window closes June 29, 2026. This follows the March 3, 2026 FDA action issuing 30 warning letters to telehealth companies for marketing compounded GLP-1s in ways that implied equivalence to FDA-approved Wegovy/Zepbound. The branded manufacturers (Novo Nordisk, Eli Lilly) are filing in parallel. As of FDA's most recent counts, more than 455 adverse-event reports have been linked to compounded semaglutide and more than 320 to compounded tirzepatide, many from dosing errors on multi-dose vials. The window for cheap compounded GLP-1 telehealth is closing — and that has narrative leverage for the recovery/longevity peptide angle (BPC, TB-500, GHK-Cu) where compounding is moving the opposite direction.

**3. GHK-Cu copper peptides are at peak TikTok virality.** Search interest for "copper peptide before and after" has dominated skincare-side biohacker content for the last two quarters. The community is conflating topical serums (defensible, moderate evidence base, low risk) with injectable GHK-Cu sourced from research-chemical sites (not defensible, no human RCTs, sourcing risk). The classic Pickart-lineage data — that GHK-Cu increased collagen synthesis in a higher percentage of subjects than vitamin C or retinoic acid in a 1990s-era comparison — is being recycled without nuance. The opportunity: be the source that *separates* topical from injectable, cites Pickart's actual mechanism papers, and stops the conflation. The risk: most affiliate competitors are leaning into the conflation because it sells injectable kits.
