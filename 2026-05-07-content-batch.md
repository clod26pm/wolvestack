## 🔍 Trend Report

**Top 3 most timely findings:**

1. **The FDA's peptide-review schedule just got more concrete — and longer.** Reporting from the Drexel News Blog (May 5, 2026) and Science News confirms the agency is now planning a two-stage advisory cycle: a two-day Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) meeting on July 23–24, 2026 covering seven peptides (including BPC-157), with a *second* panel taking up five additional peptides by February 2027. This is a meaningful shift from the prior framing of a single July decision point. Practical impact: any expectation that Category-2 reclassification settles cleanly this summer is now wrong. The full reopened compounding lane (assuming PCAC clears the candidates) won't be operative on the 503A bulks list for at least one full calendar year, and 503B outsourcing-facility access for these peptides is even further out. Consumer takeaway: physician-judgment 503A access remains the operative pathway through mid-2027 minimum.

2. **A 2025 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial published in *Sleep Medicine* reports CJC-1295/ipamorelin administered at bedtime increased slow-wave (deep) sleep duration by 23% versus placebo (p<0.01).** This is one of the very few human RCTs in the entire growth-hormone-releasing peptide class, and it lands in an underserved niche: sleep is the single most-discussed wellness metric on biohacker TikTok and r/biohackers, but the GH-peptide conversation has been overwhelmingly dominated by body-composition framing. The sleep angle is wide open for content. Caveat: the peptide pair carries the same theoretical cancer-risk concern that Eric Topol flagged in his July 2025 *Ground Truths* "Peptide Craze" essay (broad cellular-growth signaling); the slow-wave-sleep result is provocative but should not be presented as a free lunch.

3. **The tirzepatide "muscle preservation" story is fracturing.** A medRxiv preprint posted April 11, 2026 (Greater lean-body-mass decline with tirzepatide than [comparator]) and a March 2026 *Nutrients* secondary analysis (Short-Term Associations Between Fat-Free Mass Preservation and Glycaemic Markers During Tirzepatide Therapy) together push back on the previously dominant narrative that GLP-1/GIP dual agonists preserve lean mass roughly as well as diet-and-exercise weight loss. The newer data suggest the lean-mass loss may be larger than initially modeled — particularly without concurrent resistance training and ≥1.2 g/kg protein intake. This is the highest-engagement medical-content angle on weight-loss TikTok in 2026, and the existing pillar narrative ("75% fat / 25% lean") is now contested rather than settled. Strong opportunity for a sober explainer.
