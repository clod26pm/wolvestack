Public comment window closes June 29. *Sentiment angle:* the audience that started on $200/mo compounded sema is now staring down a forced migration to $1,000+/mo brand-name Wegovy/Zepbound, and they're looking for either (a) ways to taper off or (b) adjacent peptides that fit the same metabolic goals. This is a real content gap — and a real lead-magnet opportunity. ([Pharmacy Times coverage](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-moves-to-permanently-close-the-door-on-compounded-glp-1s), [Orrick analysis](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/05/FDA-Moves-to-Shut-the-Door-on-Large-Scale-Compounding-of-GLP1-Drugs), [FDA statement](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-clarifies-policies-compounders-national-glp-1-supply-begins-stabilize))

3. **The "evidence vs enthusiasm" gap on BPC-157 is widening and the smart corners of Reddit are starting to call it out.** Community sentiment on r/peptides remains "miraculous" for tendon work, but the meta-discussion has shifted — there are zero published human RCTs on BPC-157 to date, and the recurring objection is that most users stacking BPC-157 are also doing PT, deloading, and sleeping more, then attributing 100% of recovery to the peptide. The PCAC briefing documents will almost certainly weigh exactly this question. *Sentiment angle:* there is a real content opening for honest, research-language coverage that doesn't oversell — the audience trusts the few voices willing to say "the animal data is striking, the human data is thin, here is what we actually know." ([Peptide Authority Reddit roundup](https://peptideauthority.co.uk/blog/most-popular-peptides-reddit-2026))
